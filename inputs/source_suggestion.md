# Source suggestions

Editable guidance for finding sources for each supplied research domain.

## Official and primary sources

- Investigate relevant municipal/local, state/regional and national government websites: planning authorities, public records, regulators, official statistics, legislation and public notices.
- For the subject's actual place, investigate relevant council and parliamentary papers,
  adopted/proposed budgets, procurement and investment programmes, development/use plans,
  official demographic/employment series, hazard maps and utility/transport infrastructure
  plans. Use public defense or military estate records when the occupier, nearby activity
  or infrastructure makes them relevant; never seek restricted information.
- Seek primary company publications relevant to the subject: issuer filings, annual reports, official announcements, operator updates, manufacturer documentation and infrastructure plans. Attribute company claims; a large company is not automatically an independent or reliable authority.
- Include central banks, regulated exchanges, standards bodies and original research publications when they support a domain responsibility.
- Match jurisdiction, geography, sector and period to the supplied subject. Search in the local language where it helps locate the governing record.
- Prefer specific records, datasets, notices and documents over generic homepages. A useful official portal can remain a navigation source when no direct record is available.
- Use reputable secondary sources where they add necessary coverage or a primary record is unavailable; explain that role in the source description.

## Relevance and access

- Cover the domain's substantive research needs, including named entities, systems, counterparties and dependencies that require outside information.
- Investigate relevant official sources, but do not invent sources or force an unrelated government or company entry into every domain. No fixed source count or website whitelist applies.
- Attempt to open every proposed URL and report what was actually readable through the available web tool. A search result, snippet or citation alone is not an access test.
- Distinguish readable content, partial content, explicit access restrictions and failed or inconclusive opening attempts. Do not infer a paywall or bot block merely from a failed request.
- Keep useful document links even when they require later processing. Do not assume PDFs are unreadable or that HTML pages are readable.
- During discovery, return sources and concise reasons for relevance. During research,
  follow these same authority and jurisdiction preferences while reading underlying evidence.
- If a URL fails, search its exact title, publisher and subject in the relevant language.
  Prefer the original publisher or an authoritative replacement, open the exact returned
  URL and check jurisdiction, subject and period. Do not reconstruct paths, blindly pick
  the first result or assume a replacement is equivalent. Keep failed attempts and
  alternative sources distinct; disclose genuine access limitations.
