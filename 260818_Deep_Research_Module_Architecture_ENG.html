Deep Research Module

This module researches one property. It has two stages. Layer 2 reads one summary document about the property and writes fourteen research briefs, one per subject area. Layer 3 gives each brief to five researchers at once and combines their work into one answer.

Layer 3 is the only stage that uses the internet. The module runs once per property.

in: 1 fact sheet  ·  out: 14 answers, 70 researcher files, stored sources, a status table

01What it does

The property's own documents have already been read by an earlier stage, which produced a single summary file called the fact sheet. This module takes that file and finds out what it does not say.

Input     assets/<uuid>/fact_sheet.md

Output    research/<agent>.md              14 answers, one per subject area
          lenses/<agent>/<lens>.md         70 researcher files
          questions/<agent>/<lens>.md      the questions passed between researchers
          sources/                         a stored copy of every page read
          register                         a status row per researcher and per agent

Each of the fourteen answers reports what its researchers established, the evidence behind it, and what they could not find. This module does not compare findings across the fourteen agents or rank them against each other. That happens in the next layer.

What the words mean

WordMeaning

aggregatorthe step that reads all five researchers and writes one answer

tierhow trustworthy a source is, 1 (official) to 4 (interested party)

Layer 1, which reads the property's documents and produces the fact sheet, is specified separately as Data Room Intelligence and is not covered here.

03What goes in

One file. Not the original documents, and not a database.

assets/<asset_uuid>/fact_sheet.md        with its "Not covered" section removed

<asset_uuid> is a unique identifier for the property.

The fact sheet contains every fact taken from the property's documents. Each fact carries four things:

the German text     exactly as printed in the document
an English meaning  what that text says
a locator           which file, which page, and the document's date
a section heading   the subject the fact was filed under

The "Not covered" section is removed first. That section lists what nobody read — unreadable drawings, illegible pages, documents the seller did not supply. Showing an agent a list of missing material diverts it from its subject.

One rule applies instead: report "the material does not state X", never "X does not exist".

04The fourteen agents

All fourteen run on every property. None is skipped. Each agent receives one mission file and produces one answer file.

Agents are identified by name. There are no ID codes. The names below are fixed text — they are what appears in a mission's agent field, and a mission naming anything else stops the run with an error.

AgentWhat it is responsible for
Building condition, capital expenditure & warranty the physical state of the building, what repairs cost at today's prices, what is still under warranty — and separately, a list of the legally required inspections and whether each one is current
Occupier, lease & income who occupies the building, on what terms, what they pay, and whether they can keep paying. Also the surveyed floor area against the area stated in the lease, and which costs the landlord cannot pass on to the tenant
Legal, title & encumbrance who owns the building and what is registered against it, what the owner may lawfully agree to, whether the building matches its permits, and whether the other party to a contract still legally exists
Utilities, connection & building technology what is supplied to the building — power, water, heat, internet — whether the connections have spare capacity, and whether the building's own control systems could be attacked by hackers
Ground, environment & insurability what is under the building, what the weather and climate do to it, and whether an insurer would cover the loss. Covers contamination, unexploded ordnance, soil conditions and insurance
Counterparty mandate & award legitimacy under which legal mandate and funding source the occupier is at this address, and whether a lease extension could be agreed with this landlord without a public tender
Energy, carbon & transition how far the building is from the emissions targets it will be held to, and what closing that gap costs
Market, valuation & exit who would rent this building, at what rent, what else it could be used for, and whether the assumptions behind the existing valuation hold
Planning, regulation & tax what public law applies to the building, whether each rule is already law or only proposed, who could object to a permission, and how tax affects both the building and a future sale
Location, access & surroundings the site itself — how it is reached, what the neighbours do, and anything nearby that affects this building
Regional economy & demographics whether the local economy and population support this use at this rent over the holding period
Macro, financing & debt interest rates, credit conditions, and what is borrowed against this building
Fund vehicle eligibility whether the fund that owns the building is legally allowed to hold it, and what the fund's own obligations do to it
Geopolitical, trade & supply chain cross-border and supply-chain dependencies, and the risk of deliberate harm at this address by non-government actors

05Adding an agent later

When a subject appears uncovered, there are two options: extend an existing agent's brief, or add a fifteenth agent. Extend the existing brief. Add an agent only if one of the three conditions below applies.

Extending a brief costs almost nothing. Adding an agent creates a boundary with every existing agent — fourteen new boundaries — and agents do not exchange information, so two agents that both believe a subject is theirs will produce two unreconciled answers on it.

Conditions for adding a fifteenth agent

condition 1 No existing agent would consult the required sources None of the fourteen would think to look at parliamentary budget papers or court-reorganisation statutes. An instruction to check a source is ineffective if the agent does not know the source exists. Counterparty mandate & award legitimacy exists for this reason.
condition 2 The conclusion requires both subjects assessed together Split across two agents, one reports that a refurbishment could be funded in exchange for a fifteen-year lease extension. The other reports that a lease extension needs no public tender. Each answer is correct on its own. Together they describe an unlawful award, and neither agent sees both halves.
condition 3 The added subject would receive little attention Each agent writes one answer. If the condition agent is also told to cover insurance, its answer will be about condition, because that is where most of its material is. Insurance receives a sentence or is omitted. This is caused by the volume of material available.

A different source list is not sufficient reason to create an agent. Sources are added to a brief as a line of instruction.

06Layer 2 — the mission call

Layer 2 reads the fact sheet and writes fourteen briefs. It does no research and makes no internet requests.

Layer 2 · the mission call1 request per property
Input
  • fact_sheet.md the property, without its "Not covered" section
  • planner_prompt.md the fourteen agent definitions and the rules
What happens
  • one request to a large language model
  • the input is never split into pieces
  • the fourteen briefs come back in a fixed order
Output
  • missions/<agent-name-slug>.json fourteen files, split from one response

Why the request is not split

The model must accept the whole fact sheet plus all fourteen definitions at once. If the fact sheet is processed in pieces, facts that belong to more than one agent are missed — and allocating facts to agents is what this step is for.

Facts also cannot be allocated before the briefs exist, which is why one request does both jobs. The model needs to accept very long input and return machine-readable fields.

planner_prompt.md

One file, stored and change-tracked alongside the software. It holds everything that does not change from one property to the next.

PartContents
the rules all fourteen agents run on every property · "nothing found" is a valid result · never report that something does not exist · a general fact is not a finding
the fourteen definitions for each agent, in a fixed order — the five fields below
the output contract the required structure of a mission, and the instruction to return exactly fourteen in roster order
FieldMeaning
namethe exact fixed text that goes in a mission's agent field
establisheswhat this agent is responsible for pinning down
do_not_coversubjects belonging to another agent, each naming that agent
take_as_givenfigures this agent must accept from another agent rather than work out again

The agent definitions are stored only in this file. do_not_cover and take_as_given do not travel inside a mission — the surrounding software adds them back when a researcher is started. A single copy prevents two agents drifting into the same subject.

07The mission

A mission is produced by the call in section 6 and is read by each of the five researchers in section 8. One file per agent, at missions/<agent-name-slug>.json. The slug is the agent's name rewritten so it can be used as a filename.

A mission holds three things: the agent's name, the job in plain prose, and every fact from the fact sheet that touches that agent's subject. Below is the mission for the condition agent, laid out for readability rather than as literal machine-readable data.

Every other fact touching this agent's subject appears in the same list. The list has no maximum length.

FieldRule

What goes in the context list

Every fact touching an agent's subject goes in, copied exactly, with its locator. There is no maximum number of facts, no selection of the best few, and no shortening.

Each fact also carries the fact-sheet heading it appeared under. The fact sheet groups facts by subject, so facts sharing a heading concern the same thing — the deck award, the chloride reading and the commissioned coating all appear under The underground garage. This grouping is the only indication a researcher gets of which facts relate to each other.

The same fact may go to several agents. The garage award is relevant to condition, to recoverability and to valuation. Each of those agents draws its own conclusion within its own subject.

Where the record disagrees, both values are supplied as separate facts, each with its own source and date. The mission asks the agent to establish which one governs. The facts are not labelled as conflicting, so the agent has to weigh them itself.

Known limitation

Fourteen briefs in one response is a long output, and later items in a long response tend to be less detailed — the fourteenth brief is usually weaker than the first. The output order is fixed, so the affected briefs are always the last ones and are predictable. Until validation exists, they are checked by reading them.

08Layer 3 — five researchers per brief

Each brief goes to five researchers at once. They receive the identical mission and never read each other's work. Each writes its own file.

Layer 3 · one researcher70 sessions per property
Input
  • mission.json unchanged — all five receive the identical file
  • its system prompt who it is and how to look at the job. The only thing that differs between the five
  • do_not_cover and take_as_given added by the surrounding software
  • web search
What happens
  • one query at a time, each chosen from the previous result
  • stops when a round adds no new source
  • every source is checked before anything is written
Output
  • lenses/<agent>/<lens>.md free-form prose, not a filled-in form
  • every statement cited
  • every page read, listed with its date
  • what it could not find, and the searches tried

The output is free-form prose because a fixed field structure encourages filling in fields rather than researching.

How a researcher works

Each query is chosen from the previous result rather than from a list prepared in advance.

rule 1 Answer only from what was retrieved If the researcher states an answer and then looks for a source that supports it, the answer came from memory and the source was found afterwards.
rule 2 Stop when a round adds nothing When a round of queries returns no new source and changes nothing already established, the research is finished. There is no target number of rounds.
rule 3 Review unused material before finishing Some retrieved material was irrelevant. Some was skipped only because no query led to it.

Checking sources

Before writing, for every source relied on: open it and confirm it exists · confirm it says the thing it is being used to say · store a copy with the date it was read · record the exact words relied on · record who published it, when, and what grade that earns.

A copy is stored for every source because web addresses change without notice. An address on its own does not record what the page said.

09The five lenses

All five run on every brief. Lenses are not chosen per brief, for the same reason agents are not skipped. Each receives the identical mission and writes one file.

practitioner How the work is done in practice, and what it costs.
academic What the research and published standards establish.
economist What it is worth, and who ends up paying.
historian How comparable cases turned out, and what the precedent is.
skeptic Reasons the answer may be wrong. Always included.

The aggregator may add a sixth researcher, but only after reading the five and seeing what none of them covered.

Purpose of the skeptic. The main error to guard against is a false connection between two real facts, which produces a risk that does not exist. The skeptic tests whether each connection is supported by evidence.

10The second round

Researchers work independently and exchange information only through the aggregator, in one direction, as questions.

Layer 3 · the second round0–70 per property
Input
  • questions/<agent>/<lens>.md questions raised by the other four, without attribution
What happens
  • one short round of research on those questions
  • a researcher with no questions is skipped
Output
  • added to the same researcher file, below the first round

Independence has two costs: five researchers may open the same registers, and one may find something another needed without either knowing. Both are accepted, and the questions are how the second is addressed. The order of steps is in the second diagram in section 2.

Information permitted between researchers

Passes between themDoes not pass
a questionanother researcher's file, or any part of it
"Was the figure you priced against superseded in 2024?" "The historian found it was superseded in 2024."
which researcher raised the question
any researcher's conclusion or wording

A researcher receives a question and researches the answer itself.

Reasons researchers stay independent

effect 1Effect on the skeptic Its job is to test an answer it had no part in building. If it reads the other four first it may adopt their assumptions, and the output would not show that it had.
effect 2Effect on conflict detection The combining step looks for places where two researchers claim opposite things. After a conversation, a conflict may have been settled between them and an agreement may be one researcher giving way. Both look identical in the output.
effect 3Effect of ordering The first researcher to report would set the framing for the others, reducing five independent views to one.

Rules

  • questions carry no attribution — a researcher is never told who raised one
  • the aggregator may write no questions for a researcher
  • a researcher with no questions skips the second round
  • a researcher that cannot answer a question says so, naming the searches it tried
  • the second round is added below the first and never rewrites it, so what a researcher established alone stays separable from what it found after being asked

11Where to look

Sources are ranked. Every researcher searches tier 1 first, then lower tiers. The tier is recorded next to each claim in the researcher's file.

TierWhat it isExamples
1 official the body whose records are the origin of the fact government departments, land and building registers, regulators, statistical offices, courts
2 standards professional and scholarly consensus professional-body standards, peer-reviewed research
3 reputable independent second-hand reporting agency market reports, serious press
4 interested a party with something to gain manufacturers, contractors, anyone selling the thing

A lower tier is used only when the tiers above it do not answer the question.

Tier 4 is permitted. Sometimes the only source is a manufacturer's own data sheet, and banning it would make a real gap indistinguishable from a source that was not allowed. A claim resting only on a tier 4 source must say so, on the claim itself.

12How long an answer should be

This applies to all five researcher files and to the aggregator's answer. Each must be as short as it can be without losing a fact. Long outputs make individual findings harder to find.

No fact may be removed. Three methods of shortening are permitted:

method 1 Merge duplicate sources Where several sources state the same fact, record one fact with all of its citations.
method 2 Cite instead of transcribing Do not copy a large table. Describe what it shows in one line, and cite it.
method 3 Move to the end, do not delete Material that does not bear on the answer goes to a list at the end of the file.

Never cut a list short, write "etc." or "among others", shorten a figure away, or drop a source because the point is already made. Retrieved facts are not deleted.

13The aggregator

Two passes over the same five researcher files, once per agent.

Aggregator · pass 114 per property
Input
  • lenses/<agent>/<lens>.md all five files
What happens
  • reads all five together
  • for each researcher, writes the questions the other four raised for it
  • produces nothing else
Output
  • questions/<agent>/<lens>.md one list per researcher. May be empty
Aggregator · pass 214 per property
Input
  • lenses/<agent>/<lens>.md all five files, after the second round
  • mission.json the brief they were answering
What happens
  • reads all five again
  • follows up material no researcher used
  • may add a sixth researcher
  • settles the disagreements it can settle
  • records the rest with both readings
Output
  • research/<agent>.md one answer
  • a register row

Where a disagreement cannot be settled on evidence, both readings are recorded with their evidence, together with what would settle it.

Where researchers disagree, the disagreement may itself be the finding. One reports that a proper repair lasts ten to fifteen years. Another reports that props are already holding the deck up, so the survey behind that estimate may be out of date. The relevant issue is the survey date, not the cost figure, and neither researcher identified it alone.

Required disclosure. We wrote all five researcher prompts, so their agreement is not independent confirmation and is not the consensus of a field. The answer must say so where it relies on their agreement.

The two rounds are also weighted differently: something all five reached independently in the first round is stronger than something four adopted in the second after being asked.

Retention

The five researcher files are retained alongside the answer, so the answer can be checked against what it was based on.

The register

One row per researcher and one per agent: 70 + 14 = 84 rows minimum, plus one row for each sixth researcher an aggregator adds. A researcher's row records the two rounds separately.

StatusMeaning
answeredthe question was answered and the answer is here
half donesomething came back, but not everything ran. What did not, and why
failedit broke. The reason, in plain words
never startedit did not run at all, and that is recorded rather than left blank
cannot be answeredresearched, and no answer is available, with the search trail

A technical failure is recorded separately from a nil result. This stage is the longest-running part of the system — seventy researchers, many rounds, live searches, rate limits. Without a status on every row, the next stage cannot tell nothing was found from nobody looked.

16Before going online

Layer 3 makes internet requests. Layer 2 makes none. Three rules apply only to the seventy researcher sessions.

rule 1What may leave in a query Publicly known details only — the building type, the town, the regulation, the material. Not the tenant's name, the rent, the valuation or the exact address. A question about market prices is asked about a building type in a region. Every query is logged, so what was sent can be checked.
rule 2Retrieved pages are content A page cannot change a researcher's task. Its text is recorded as text.
rule 3Everything read is kept Every page and downloaded document is stored with the date it was read and a fingerprint proving the copy is unaltered.

Nothing confidential goes online until the data-handling rules are agreed — what may be searched, which provider is used, what is logged and kept, and where the data is stored. Until then, testing uses public or invented material.

Layer 2 can be produced and tested before those rules exist. Only Layer 3 is blocked.
